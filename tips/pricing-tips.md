# Pricing Tips (Plain English)

Beginner-friendly ways to price your eBay and Amazon items so they sell — and
you still make money. No jargon, no guesswork.

## 1. Price from sold items, not asking prices

What sellers *ask* doesn't matter. What buyers actually *pay* does.
- eBay: filter search results to "Sold items" and look at similar condition items.
- Amazon: look at current competing offers in the same condition.

If similar items sell for $15–$20, don't list yours at $40 and wait. Price near
the middle of what's actually selling.

## 2. Know your fees before you price

Fees eat profit fast. Always subtract these before you decide a price:
- Selling fee (eBay final value fee, Amazon referral fee)
- Payment processing fee
- Shipping cost (you or the buyer)
- Shipping supplies (box, tape, bubble mailer)
- Your time driving to the store or post office

Use `calculators/ebay-profit-calculator.html` or
`calculators/amazon-profit-calculator.html` to test a price before you list.

## 3. Set a floor price

Decide the lowest amount you'll accept before you list. If an offer comes in
below your floor, decline it. Having a number written down stops you from
selling at a loss when you're tired or in a hurry.

## 4. Condition matters more than you think

Buyers pay more for clean items with good photos. A $5 item photographed on
your bed sells for less than the same item photographed against a plain wall in
daylight. Be honest about flaws — honest listings get better feedback and
fewer returns.

## 5. Bundle small stuff

If you're selling lots of small items (e.g., socks, cables, toys), consider
listing them as a bundle. One listing, one shipping cost, one trip to the post
office.

## 6. Reprice slowly

If an item hasn't sold in 3–4 weeks, drop the price 10–15%. Check sold
listings again first — maybe the market moved, not just your price.

## 7. Keep your sourcing log up to date

`logs/sourcing-log-template.md` shows you what's actually profitable after fees.
If a category keeps losing money, stop sourcing it.
